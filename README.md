# Architecture Principles

This repository contains the enduring principles that guide architectural decision-making.

Architectural principles act as positive constraints. Without being prescriptive, they simplify decision-making by clarifying:

- What will always be true.
- What will never be true.

The principles are used to:

- Inform data and digital strategy and investment planning.
- Direct architectural policies, standards and guidance.
- Support technology professionals and suppliers in making sound design decisions.

## Philosophy

Principles are not rules; rules tell people what to do.

Principles help people make good decisions when the rules do not provide an answer.

The value of principles lies not in strict compliance but in providing a consistent basis for judgement, trade-offs and decision-making across a complex and changing organisation.

## Guidance Hierarchy

Principles form the highest level of architecture guidance.

A principle is a strategic statement of intent and represents a commitment by senior leadership to pursue a particular direction for investment and development.

Principles are:

- Enduring
- Complementary
- Non-hierarchical

There will inevitably be trade-offs when applying principles. In some circumstances designs may not fully comply with every principle; such decisions should be made consciously and with appropriate governance.

Principles are supported by lower levels of guidance:

### Principles

Strategic guidance that establishes the overall direction of development and investment.

### Conventions (Policies)

Detailed statements of intent that support one or more principles.

### Standards

Mandatory standards, patterns and procedures that support conventions.

# List of Principles

Individual organisations may choose their own principles but below are a list of what we as architects believe are a good set of principles which should apply everywhere. This list is not intended to be specific to any organisation but are a set that can be adopted or adapted, in part or in whole.  Contributors are encouraged to add more principles to this list, even where they are in opposition to ones already on the list because what is true in one context may not be the case in others.
